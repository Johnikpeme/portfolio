The problem

Hugo runs CX programs for many clients: Gift Health, Earnest RCM, Almedia, Aurora and more. The numbers lived in spreadsheets, several BI tools and email reports. Leadership, ops and SLA teams each had their own version of the truth, and month-end reviews turned into reconciliation exercises.

What I built

  • Performance Control Centers for every client program: Daily, WBR and MBR views with drill-downs into any agent or team lead, plus editable grading targets.
  • SLA analytics in one view: violations, compliance, audit outcomes, breach durations, top offenders, weekly and shift trends, fully filterable.
  • Governed manual entry for Management KPIs, CX Headcount, Billable Hours and Billable HC, with audit history and owner gates.
  • A machine-learning forecasting engine for agent-level P&L, with human-override anchors for edge cases.
  • Automated invoicing tied to billable hours, org chart and workforce planning, scheduling, QA scorecards, and embedded Omni dashboards behind SSO.

Impact & decisions

  • One place for the numbers. The spreadsheet, BI-tool and email sprawl it replaced is retired.
  • A Data Trust layer holds every KPI definition and a decisions log, so arguments are about the business, not the formula.
  • Forecasts stay overridable by humans on purpose: an anchor beats a silently wrong model when a client changes scope mid-month.

The problem

The bank was paying a recurring licence for a SaaS service desk that didn't fit how the technology teams actually worked. Incidents, changes and problems were tracked in slightly different ways by different groups, and the licence bill grew with every seat.

What I built

  • Proposed the project, got sponsorship, and led it from module design to rollout.
  • Modules for Incidents, Service Requests, Changes, Releases, Problem Management and Post-Implementation Reviews: one workflow across all technology functions.
  • Built on a GLPI-derived PHP/JS core rather than from scratch, so the team could ship quickly and own every line afterwards.
  • Figma prototypes first, then the build, so stakeholders signed off on flows before code.

Impact & decisions

  • $150,000+ saved every year in licence costs, with the platform fully owned in-house.
  • One set of definitions for incident priority, change risk and problem ownership across the bank.
  • Trade-off accepted: an in-house tool means in-house maintenance. That is why it was built on a mature open core rather than a bespoke framework.

The problem

Tickets arrived from FreshService and were routed by hand, often to the wrong team first. When a channel went down, engineers spent the first stretch of every incident working out what category of failure they were even looking at. Customer sentiment lived in spreadsheets nobody had time to clean.

What I built

  • A supervised ticket-classification model trained on FreshService data that categorises and routes tickets automatically.
  • A downtime model that predicts the root-cause error and a description for an incident from its first signals.
  • OneInsight: a unified platform for sentiment and location analysis that gives the ITSM team instant insights instead of a data-cleaning job.
  • A transaction-volume predictor and automated Python ingestion for NIBSS market data; weekly reports went from hours to minutes.

Impact & decisions

  • Ticket resolution times down 55%; MTTR on incidents improved 50%; sentiment data-prep time down 95%.
  • Kept the models explainable. Analysts see the top categories and probabilities, not a single opaque label, so they trust and correct them.
  • Routing runs inside the ITSM flow via Power Automate, so adoption needed no new tool to learn.

05 / About

Petroleum engineer by degree, data person by choice.

I found data in my final year at Covenant University and never went back to the rigs.

Since then I've run the same loop over and over: find the question, get the data, build the model, then build the thing people actually open in the morning. In a bank that meant real-time monitoring, an ITSM platform and ML models the ITSM team trusts. At Hugo it means one Intelligence Hub for CX, finance and operations across every client.

I also co-founded Dash Studios, a Lagos game studio, to prove the loop works outside a bank. Nouns Hunt hit the App Store's top 12 word games within nine hours of its beta and grew to 20,000+ players in 65 countries, guided by the same data habits.

What I care about most: the decision a dashboard changes, not the dashboard. Models people can read and override. Interfaces designed with the data model, not after it.

Off hours I'm a football fan, a gamer, and a dog person. I share updates and the occasional banter on X.
